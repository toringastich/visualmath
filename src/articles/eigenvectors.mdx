## A matrix moves every vector

Here is a matrix, M, and what it does to the plane. The grid you see isn't
decoration — it *is* space, and M is warping all of it. The vector v = (1, 0)
rides along. Press play on the matrix row and watch where v lands.

  state={EIGEN_RIDE}
  caption="M warps the plane; v = (1, 0) rides the warp. Press ▶ on the matrix row."
  height={460}
/>

Almost every vector gets knocked off its own line. v started pointing due
east; it lands pointing somewhere new. Rotate the matrix's entries and the
story repeats — direction in, *different* direction out. That's what a matrix
does. It moves everything.

Almost everything.

## The vectors that refuse to turn

Type `eigen(M)` and Warp draws two dashed lines through the origin. They look
unremarkable. They are not: they are the only two lines in the entire plane
that the warp leaves *in place*.

<WarpEmbed
  state={EIGEN_LINES}
  caption="eigen(M) draws the invariant lines. Play the warp — the orange and purple vectors stretch but never turn."
  height={460}
/>

A vector on one of those lines doesn't get turned when M applies — it only
gets stretched, by a factor the readout calls λ. Play the animation and watch
the orange and purple vectors: space swirls around them, and they simply…
lengthen. Those are the **eigenvectors** of M, and the stretch factors λ are
its **eigenvalues**.

## See it with your own vector

Don't take the dashed lines' word for it. Here v = (1, 1) sits exactly on an
eigen-line, and the row u = M·v computes where it lands: (3, 3). Same
direction. Three times the length. λ = 3, in the flesh.

<WarpEmbed
  state={EIGEN_ON_LINE}
  caption="v = (1, 1) lies on an eigen-line, so M·v = (3, 3) — stretched by λ = 3, never turned."
  height={460}
/>

Now nudge v off the line — edit it to (1, 0.8) — and watch u swing away from
v's direction. Put it back and they re-align. The eigen-line is a knife edge,
and you can feel it.

That is the entire idea. *An eigenvector of M is a vector that M stretches
but does not turn; the eigenvalue is the stretch.* Everything else — the
characteristic polynomial, det(M − λI) = 0, the algebra you'll grind through
on the exam — is the bookkeeping you do to *find* the vectors this article
just showed you.

## Now it's yours

The sandbox below is the full version of Warp — the same tool every scene in
this article ran on. Some experiments worth your time:

- `eigen` of a **rotation** matrix: nothing survives a turn, so there are no
  real eigenvectors at all. Warp reports the complex pair.
- `eigen` of a **shear**: exactly one direction survives. Repeated eigenvalue,
  one eigen-line.
- `eigen` of the **identity**: everything survives. Every vector is an
  eigenvector.
